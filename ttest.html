Does Your Writing Hand Beat Your Other Hand?

A classroom comparison of writing speed, hand preference, and the script-writing question.

  • 9 students
  • 30-second writing task
  • Three t-tests

The study

What We Did

Our class counted how many times each student could write “I am amazing” in 30 seconds using their writing hand and their other hand. We also recorded each student's answer to the question about whether they write in script. We compared the class's writing-hand mean with Dr. Jacques's benchmark count of 8. We used t-tests to compare the benchmark, the yes and no groups, and each student's two hand counts.

The numbers

My Results

Each count represents how many times a student wrote the phrase during the timed task.

Class results across the three t-tests
Test Mean 1 Mean 2 Difference p-value
One-sample (writing hand vs. benchmark) 6.333 8.000 −1.667 0.00105
Independent-samples (Yes vs. No; Welch) 6.400 6.250 0.150 0.84808
Paired-samples (writing hand vs. other hand) 6.333 2.556 3.778 0.00000647

What the tests suggest

The Three Write-Ups

  1. The class writing-hand mean was 1.667 counts below the benchmark. The result gives strong evidence that the class mean differed from the benchmark, in the lower direction.

  2. The mean for students who answered yes was 0.150 counts higher than the mean for students who answered no. This is a small observed difference, and the result gives little evidence of a group difference.

  3. Students averaged 3.778 more counts with their writing hand than with their other hand. The result gives strong evidence of a difference in the direction of higher writing-hand counts.

The takeaway

In Plain Words

As a group, students wrote the phrase more often with their writing hand than with their other hand. Their writing-hand average was below the professor's benchmark, while the yes and no groups had very similar averages.

Looking ahead

What I Would Do Differently

This was a small study with only nine students, so the results may not represent other students. The professor's benchmark came from one person, and a single timed count may not capture how someone typically writes. I would include more participants and repeat the timed task to get a more reliable comparison.
